title = 'The Redundancy of Societal Norms: Why We Must Re-examine the Purpose of Marriage'
+++

A recent, tragic case of a young woman murdered over a dowry has once again exposed a systemic rot in our society. This article is not a lecture on morality or a generic critique of societal conduct. Instead, it is an attempt to dissect a deeper, uncomfortable truth that this tragedy forces us to confront.

In Indian society, marriage has long been viewed as an foundational pillar of existence. I do not dispute its value. For many, the aspiration to build a family, find a companion, and raise children is a deeply held dream—a life milestone that everyone wishes to achieve, and one that society frequently pressures us to fulfill. There is undeniable wisdom in companionship; a life entirely devoid of it can be profoundly isolating. In many ways, marriage and children are viewed as the traditional engines that keep the journey of life moving forward.
But let us examine this realistically in the context of the modern world. Is the purpose of human life merely to "keep going"? Are we capable of nothing more than simply dragging our feet through a predetermined existence?
While a supportive family and a loving partner are undeniable blessings, we must ask ourselves: at what cost? Can men and women, bound strictly by societal coercion, ever truly evolve? Can they ever realize their actual potential?

Women today must stop viewing marriage as an absolute mandate imposed by tradition or parents. Instead, the primary focus must shift toward financial independence, self-actualization, and carving out an individual identity. As a generation, we must recognize that our value lies in contributing meaningfully to the world, rather than allowing ourselves to become victims of a social structure that will not step in to save us. We are human beings with immense potential, not mere ornaments designed to accessorize someone else's life. The brutal truth is that no one is coming to rescue you. You must become your own hero.

Every day, we hear harrowing accounts from friends, family, and strangers about the horrors they endure within toxic relationships and marriages. When will we awaken from this collective delusion?

Marriage itself is not inherently evil; when built on the right foundation, it is a beautiful bond. However, it should never be pursued blindly out of desperation or duty. You should step into marriage only after you have constructed your own foundation. We must teach young girls to build their careers, their self-worth, and their dignity before they ever contemplate a wedding.

Most importantly, society must stop treating marriage as a mandatory checkbox. Personal growth and self-actualization should be the ultimate goals of any human being. Without them, life cannot be experienced in its fullest capacity. The current dysfunction we see in desperate relationships is only producing broken, unfulfilled individuals who are too drained to contribute significantly to the world.

Every young woman deserves to grow into a fully realized human being before she ever dreams of partnership. She must learn to stand firmly on her own feet, anchored by her own strength and dignity, before she takes on the responsibility of nurturing another life. This cultural shift is urgent, and it begins with every single woman of this generation.
