Every human being is driven by desire. We crave a great career, the accumulation of wealth, and the intoxicating weight of power and social influence. We look for specific relationships—marriage, children, a home to call our own—or chase more materialistic goals: a cutting-edge gadget, a seven-figure bank account, or a luxury automobile. The list of human desire is endless. Often, these desires are merely external imprints, absorbed from the society that surrounds us, or inherited from the cultural and familial environments of our youth. They range from the remarkably simple to the extraordinarily complex, dictating whether they are easily achieved or out of reach. 

Yet, beneath these socially conditioned ambitions and basic survival instincts lies something innate within the human spirit—something entirely independent of societal pressure or cultural indoctrination. It is a profound longing that refuses to be quieted or withered away. This subconscious and ambiguous yearning quietly governs a person's choices across a lifetime. While this core drive exists universally, its manifestation is deeply distinctive. 

A young woman who has never seen a mountain in her life finds herself by an indescribable urge to live among the peaks. A career-driven woman sacrifices her personal life for her professional status and financial success, entirely unaware that her relentless ambition is merely a subconscious deeper desire to be free. A house wife living a monotonous life of daily routines — cleaning, doing laundary, washing dishes and cooking for her joint family —entangles herself in an affair, perhaps to escape her boredom or simply drawn by the sheer thrill of something new. A womanizer, switching partners every sunset not fully understanding his desire to escape his daunting, hollowing loneliness. Take Majnu, driving himself to the brink of madness for his Laila oblivious to the truth that it is not Laila herself he seeks; rather, he craves to love with absolute, irrevocable intensity. Laila is merely the beautiful vessel through which his boundless capacity to love manifests.

This deep, aching hunger for something indefinable has no perfect word in English. But I found it in German: Sehnsucht. It is the bittersweet feeling of wanting something you cannot quite put your finger on, like being homesick for a place you have never actually been to. When we look past the surface of survival, we find that human existence is fundamentally propelled by Sehnsucht.

I resonate deeply with this concept. When I see people making choices that in conventional would not make any logical sense, I try to look deeper. It is fascinating to realize how individuals are subconsciously driven by Sehnsucht, while remaining completely unaware of it. No matter our religion, race, or culture, human beings are fundamentally the same. Our minds and emotions are universally connected. Our psyche and emotional nature are universally, almost unimaginably similar.

Sehnsucht does not mean you are unhappy or dissatisfied with your life. Of course, if one unconsciously pursues, it can lead to misery. But if you follow it with awareness, it can become the very source of one’s joy and fulfilment. When this longing finds a positive outlet, it can change life and create indelible things in the world. I believe that an artist painting a canvas, a storyteller writing a beautiful book, or a musician creating a soulful melody are all expressing their Sehnsucht. For few it maybe their Sehnsucht itself expressing in form art or perhaps a way to find their Sehnsucht. As for me, I am trying to express mine through act of writing. I am eager to see where it takes me, because truth be told, I have yet to fully comprehend what my own Sehnsucht is—or if there even exists a word for it, even in German.
